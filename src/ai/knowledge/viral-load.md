# Viral Load

## Overview
Viral load is the amount of HIV in a drop of blood. It is the most important indicator of how well ART is working. The goal of ART is to make the viral load undetectable.

## What Viral Load Means
- **Low/undetectable viral load:** The virus is suppressed. The immune system can recover. The risk of transmitting HIV is effectively zero (U=U).
- **High viral load:** The virus is actively replicating. Immune damage is ongoing. Transmission risk is high.
- **Viral load is measured in copies per millilitre (copies/mL) of blood.**

## Target: Undetectable
- **Undetectable = viral load <50 copies/mL** (the threshold of detection for standard assays in Nigeria).
- Some assays detect down to 20 or 40 copies/mL, but <50 is the standard definition.
- Once undetectable is achieved and maintained, the person is healthy and cannot sexually transmit HIV.

## Monitoring Schedule

| Time Point | Purpose |
|------------|---------|
| **Baseline** (at ART initiation) | Establish starting viral load |
| **6 months after starting ART** | Check if ART is working — should be declining |
| **12 months after starting ART** | Target: undetectable (<50 copies/mL) |
| **Annually (if stable and undetectable)** | Confirm continued suppression |
| **Every 6 months (or sooner if concerns)** | Adherence issues, clinical deterioration, change in regimen |

- After achieving undetectable, monitoring may be every 6–12 months.
- More frequent testing is done if adherence is a concern or if switching regimens.

## U=U (Undetectable = Untransmittable)
- Based on the PARTNER 1, PARTNER 2, HPTN 052, and Opposites Attract studies.
- No documented case of sexual transmission from a person with HIV who has a sustained undetectable viral load (<50 copies/mL).
- U=U applies to all sexual acts regardless of condom use.
- Important for reducing stigma and improving quality of life.
- U=U does NOT apply to breastfeeding or needle sharing — other prevention measures remain needed.

## What High Viral Load Indicates
- **Treatment failure:** ART is not working effectively
- **Poor adherence:** Missing doses allows the virus to replicate
- **Drug resistance:** The virus has mutated and is no longer fully susceptible to the current regimen
- **Recent infection:** Before ART starts, viral load is typically very high (hundreds of thousands to millions)
- **Interruption of ART:** When ART is stopped, viral load rebounds within weeks

### Clinical Implications of High Viral Load
- Faster decline in CD4 count
- Increased risk of OIs
- Increased risk of HIV transmission to others
- Disease progression

## Blips
- A "blip" is a temporary, low-level detectable viral load (usually 50–200 copies/mL) in a person who has otherwise been undetectable.
- Blips are common and usually not a cause for alarm.
- Blips often resolve on the next test without any change in treatment.
- Possible causes: lab error, intercurrent illness, vaccination, temporary adherence lapse.
- Management: Continue the same regimen; repeat viral load in 1–3 months.
- A sustained detectable viral load (>200 copies/mL on two consecutive tests) indicates possible treatment failure and requires further investigation.

## Viral Load in Special Populations
- **Infants and children:** Viral load tends to be higher. PCR testing is used for diagnosis (antibody tests are unreliable before 18 months).
- **Pregnant women:** Viral load should be undetectable at delivery to prevent mother-to-child transmission. Repeat testing in the 3rd trimester and at delivery.
- **TB co-infection:** Temporary viral load increase is possible; manage TB treatment and continue ART.

## Key Counselling Points
- Keep taking ART every day — this is how you stay undetectable.
- Undetectable means healthy immune system and zero risk of sexual transmission.
- Getting to undetectable takes time — usually 3–6 months with good adherence.
- Your viral load can go back up if you miss doses or stop treatment.
- Always check your viral load results with your healthcare provider.
