# Respiratory Conditions — Knowledge Base

## Asthma
- Chronic inflammatory disease of airways
- Symptoms: wheezing, cough (especially at night or early morning), chest tightness, shortness of breath
- Triggers: allergens (pollen, dust mites, mold), exercise, cold air, respiratory infections, smoke, strong odors
- Diagnosis: clinical history, spirometry (lung function test)
- Treatment:
  - Reliever (rescue) inhaler: salbutamol (albuterol) — for acute symptoms
  - Preventer (controller): inhaled corticosteroids (beclomethasone, budesonide) — daily use for control
  - Severe: add-on therapies like LABA, leukotriene receptor antagonists
- Asthma action plan: know your zones (green = good, yellow = caution, red = emergency)

## Pneumonia
- Infection of lung tissue
- Causes: bacteria (Streptococcus pneumoniae most common), viruses, fungi
- Symptoms: cough with phlegm (may be green, yellow, or bloody), fever, chills, chest pain, shortness of breath, fatigue
- Diagnosis: chest X-ray, blood tests, sputum culture
- Treatment: antibiotics for bacterial pneumonia, supportive care for viral
- Prevention: pneumococcal vaccine, influenza vaccine

## Bronchitis
- Inflammation of bronchial tubes
- Acute: usually viral, follows upper respiratory infection, cough with clear/yellow mucus
- Chronic: cough with mucus most days for at least 3 months, 2 years in a row — often in smokers
- Treatment: rest, fluids, cough relief. Antibiotics only if bacterial infection suspected.

## COPD (Chronic Obstructive Pulmonary Disease)
- Progressive lung disease including chronic bronchitis and emphysema
- Primary cause: smoking (90% of cases)
- Symptoms: chronic cough, sputum production, shortness of breath on exertion, wheezing
- Diagnosis: spirometry (FEV1/FVC ratio < 0.70)
- Treatment: smoking cessation (most important), bronchodilators, inhaled corticosteroids, pulmonary rehabilitation, oxygen therapy in advanced disease
- Exacerbations: worsening symptoms triggered by infections — may require antibiotics, steroids, or hospitalization

## Upper Respiratory Infections (Common Cold, Flu)
- Viral infections: rhinovirus, influenza, RSV, adenovirus
- Symptoms: runny nose, sore throat, cough, sneezing, mild fever, body aches
- Treatment: rest, hydration, symptomatic relief (paracetamol, decongestants)
- Antibiotics do NOT work for viral infections
- Influenza: more severe, high fever, body aches, fatigue — annual vaccine available

## When to Escalate
- Severe difficulty breathing or gasping for air
- Blue lips or face (cyanosis)
- Chest pain or pressure
- Coughing up blood
- High fever not responding to medication
- Confusion or drowsiness
- Unable to speak in full sentences
