# Diabetes — Knowledge Base

## Types
- Type 1: autoimmune destruction of pancreatic beta cells, absolute insulin deficiency. Usually presents in childhood or adolescence. Requires lifelong insulin.
- Type 2: insulin resistance with relative insulin deficiency. Associated with obesity, physical inactivity, family history. Managed with lifestyle, oral medications, and sometimes insulin.
- Gestational diabetes: develops during pregnancy, usually resolves after delivery. Increases risk of type 2 later in life.

## Diagnosis
- Fasting blood glucose >= 7.0 mmol/L (126 mg/dL)
- Random blood glucose >= 11.1 mmol/L (200 mg/dL) with symptoms
- HbA1c >= 6.5% (48 mmol/mol)
- Oral glucose tolerance test (OGTT): 2-hour glucose >= 11.1 mmol/L

## Symptoms
- Polyuria (frequent urination)
- Polydipsia (excessive thirst)
- Polyphagia (increased hunger)
- Unexplained weight loss
- Fatigue
- Blurred vision
- Slow-healing wounds
- Frequent infections (skin, urinary tract)

## Management
**Lifestyle:**
- Healthy diet: high fiber, vegetables, whole grains, lean protein; limit sugar and refined carbs
- Regular physical activity: 150 min/week moderate exercise
- Weight management
- No smoking
- Limit alcohol

**Medications:**
- Metformin — first-line for type 2
- Sulfonylureas (glibenclamide, glimepiride)
- DPP-4 inhibitors (sitagliptin)
- SGLT2 inhibitors (empagliflozin)
- Insulin — required for type 1, may be needed for type 2

**Monitoring:**
- Self-monitoring of blood glucose
- HbA1c every 3-6 months (target usually < 7%)
- Annual: eye exam, foot exam, kidney function, lipid profile

## Acute Complications
**Hypoglycemia (low blood sugar < 3.9 mmol/L):**
- Causes: missed meals, too much medication, excess exercise
- Symptoms: shakiness, sweating, confusion, weakness, hunger, irritability
- Treatment: 15g fast-acting glucose (glucose tablets, juice, sugar water). Recheck in 15 minutes. Repeat if still low.

**Hyperglycemia (high blood sugar):**
- Causes: missed medication, illness, overeating
- Symptoms: frequent urination, excessive thirst, fatigue
- Diabetic ketoacidosis (DKA) — more common in type 1: nausea, vomiting, abdominal pain, fruity breath, deep breathing — EMERGENCY
- Hyperosmolar hyperglycemic state (HHS) — more common in type 2: extreme dehydration, confusion — EMERGENCY

## Chronic Complications
- Cardiovascular disease (heart attack, stroke) — 2-4x higher risk
- Diabetic nephropathy (kidney disease) — leading cause of kidney failure
- Diabetic retinopathy (eye disease) — leading cause of blindness
- Diabetic neuropathy (nerve damage) — numbness, tingling, pain in feet
- Diabetic foot — ulcers, infections, risk of amputation

## Prevention (Type 2)
- Maintain healthy weight (BMI 18.5–24.9)
- Regular physical activity
- Healthy diet
- Avoid smoking
- Regular screening if high risk (family history, overweight, age > 40)
