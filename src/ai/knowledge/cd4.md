# CD4 Count

## Overview
CD4 cells (also called T-helper cells) are white blood cells that coordinate the immune system's response to infection. HIV infects and destroys CD4 cells. The CD4 count tells us how strong the immune system is. The viral load tells us how active HIV is.

## What CD4 Count Means
- CD4 count is measured in cells per cubic millimetre (cells/mm³) of blood.
- In HIV-negative adults: CD4 count is typically **500–1,500 cells/mm³**.
- In children: CD4 count is normally higher; percentages are often used for children under 5 years.

## CD4 Count Categories

| CD4 Count | Interpretation | Risk |
|-----------|---------------|------|
| **>500 cells/mm³** | Normal/healthy immune function | Low risk of OIs |
| **350–500 cells/mm³** | Mild immune suppression | Moderate risk; ART needed |
| **200–349 cells/mm³** | Significant immune suppression | Increased risk of TB, bacterial infections |
| **100–199 cells/mm³** | Severe immune suppression | High risk of PCP (pneumocystis), TB |
| **50–99 cells/mm³** | Advanced immune suppression | Very high risk of cryptococcal meningitis, toxoplasmosis |
| **<50 cells/mm³** | Critical immune suppression | Risk of MAC, CMV; AIDS-defining illnesses |

## When to Start ART
**Current WHO/Nigeria guidelines (test-and-treat):** Everyone diagnosed with HIV should start ART **regardless of CD4 count**.

Previously, ART was started based on CD4 count (e.g., <350 or <500). The test-and-treat approach started in 2016 ensures earlier treatment, better outcomes, and reduced transmission.

## CD4 Count and ART Monitoring

| Time Point | Purpose |
|------------|---------|
| **Baseline** | Assess immune status; decide on OI prophylaxis |
| **6 months after starting ART** | Check immune recovery |
| **Annually (if CD4 >350 and virally suppressed)** | Routine monitoring |
| **At viral load failure** | Assess immune decline |

**Note:** In stable patients with sustained viral suppression and CD4 >350, CD4 monitoring can be reduced. Viral load is the preferred monitoring tool for treatment success.

## When to Start OI Prophylaxis Based on CD4

| OI | CD4 Threshold for Prophylaxis |
|----|------------------------------|
| **PCP (Pneumocystis pneumonia)** | CD4 <200 cells/mm³ (or WHO Stage 3/4) |
| **Toxoplasmosis** | CD4 <100 cells/mm³ + IgG positive |
| **Cryptococcosis** | CD4 <100 cells/mm³ (screen with CrAg) |
| **MAC** | CD4 <50 cells/mm³ |

Prophylaxis can be stopped once CD4 rises above the threshold on ART (e.g., CD4 >200 for 3–6 months for PCP).

## Relationship Between CD4 and Viral Load
- **CD4 count + viral load together give the full picture:**
  - **High CD4 + Low/undetectable VL:** Healthy immune system; treatment is working.
  - **Low CD4 + High VL:** Active HIV replication; immune damage; need to start/optimize ART.
  - **Low CD4 + Low VL:** Possible advanced disease before ART; immune recovery may take time.
  - **Falling CD4 + detectable VL:** Treatment failure; check adherence and consider resistance testing.

## CD4 in Children
- Children normally have higher CD4 counts. Percentages are used for interpretation:
  - Normal CD4% in children: >35% (<12 months), >30% (1–5 years), >25% (>5 years).
  - WHO-recommends CD4% thresholds for prophylaxis in children under 5 years.
- Perinatally infected children may have been on ART for many years; CD4 recovery can be good with early treatment.

## CD4 Recovery
- On effective ART, CD4 count typically rises:
  - Rapid increase in the first 3 months (due to redistribution of memory T-cells)
  - Slower, steady increase over subsequent months/years
- Some people may not reach "normal" levels, especially if ART started very late with severely low CD4.
- Even with low CD4, viral suppression dramatically reduces OI risk.

## Key Counselling Points
- Your CD4 count tells us how strong your immune system is.
- ART helps your CD4 count go up and stay up.
- A low CD4 count means greater risk of infections — that is why you may be on preventive medicines (e.g., cotrimoxazole).
- Once your CD4 improves on ART, those preventive medicines may be stopped.
- Starting ART early (at high CD4) gives the best chance of immune recovery.
