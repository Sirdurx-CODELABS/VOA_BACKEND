# HIV (Human Immunodeficiency Virus)

## Overview
HIV is a virus that attacks the immune system, specifically CD4 cells (T-cells). Without treatment, HIV can lead to AIDS (Acquired Immunodeficiency Syndrome). With effective antiretroviral therapy (ART), people living with HIV can live long, healthy lives.

## Transmission
HIV is transmitted through:
- Unprotected vaginal or anal sex (most common route)
- Sharing contaminated needles, syringes, or other injecting equipment
- Mother-to-child transmission during pregnancy, childbirth, or breastfeeding
- Blood transfusion with contaminated blood products (rare where screening is done)
- Occupational needle-stick injuries

HIV is NOT transmitted through:
- Casual contact (hugging, shaking hands, sharing meals)
- Mosquito or insect bites
- Saliva, tears, sweat, or urine
- Sharing toilets, towels, or bedding
- Coughing or sneezing

## Testing
- **Rapid diagnostic tests (RDTs):** Finger-prick blood test, results in 15-20 minutes. First-line testing in Nigerian clinics.
- **PCR (polymerase chain reaction):** Detects viral DNA/RNA. Used for infant diagnosis (under 18 months) and early detection during window period.
- **Window period:** 2–4 weeks for 4th-generation tests; up to 3 months for some rapid tests. Testing after exposure should be repeated at 6 weeks and 3 months to confirm.
- **Confidentiality:** All HIV testing in Nigeria must follow the national policy on informed consent, counselling, and confidentiality.

## Staging
WHO Clinical Staging for HIV:
- **Stage 1:** Asymptomatic or persistent generalized lymphadenopathy
- **Stage 2:** Unexplained weight loss <10%, recurrent respiratory infections, herpes zoster, minor mucocutaneous conditions
- **Stage 3:** Unexplained weight loss >10%, unexplained chronic diarrhoea >1 month, pulmonary TB, severe bacterial infections
- **Stage 4:** HIV wasting syndrome, extrapulmonary TB, Pneumocystis pneumonia, cryptococcal meningitis, toxoplasmosis, Kaposi sarcoma

## Treatment with ART
- ART is lifelong. It does not cure HIV but suppresses the virus to undetectable levels.
- Standard first-line regimen in Nigeria: **TDF + 3TC + DTG** (Tenofovir disoproxil fumarate + Lamivudine + Dolutegravir), also called TLD as a fixed-dose combination.
- ART should be started as soon as possible after diagnosis, regardless of CD4 count (test-and-treat strategy).

## Adherence
- **≥95% adherence** is required for viral suppression. Missing doses leads to viral rebound and potential drug resistance.
- Pill burden is low — typically one pill once daily.
- Support strategies: pill boxes, phone alarms, adherence counsellors, community adherence groups.

## U=U (Undetectable = Untransmittable)
- When a person living with HIV has an undetectable viral load (<50 copies/mL), they CANNOT transmit HIV to their sexual partners.
- U=U is supported by overwhelming evidence from PARTNER 1 & 2, HPTN 052, and Opposites Attract studies.
- U=U applies only to sexual transmission. For breastfeeding and needle-sharing, other prevention measures remain important.

## Disclosure
- Disclosure is a personal decision, not a legal requirement in Nigeria (though some states have HIV disclosure laws).
- Disclosure to sexual partners is encouraged for prevention and support.
- Healthcare providers must maintain confidentiality; disclosure to third parties requires patient consent.
- Support services: disclosure counselling, peer support groups, partner notification services.

## HIV in Adolescents
- Adolescents (10–19 years) face unique challenges: puberty, sexual exploration, stigma, mental health, transition from paediatric to adult care.
- Perinatally infected adolescents may have been on ART since childhood; some may have experienced treatment interruptions or resistance.
- Adolescent-friendly services: confidential clinics, peer-led support, flexible clinic hours, youth corners in Nigerian hospitals.

## TB/HIV Co-Infection
- TB is the leading cause of death among people living with HIV.
- All people with HIV should be screened for TB at every visit; all TB patients should be tested for HIV.
- Co-management: ART (start within 2–8 weeks of TB treatment depending on CD4 count) and TB treatment (standard 6-month regimen).
- Drug interactions: Rifampicin reduces DTG levels; an additional 50 mg DTG dose is recommended when co-administered with rifampicin (or use efavirenz-based regimen).
- CPT (Cotrimoxazole Preventive Therapy) is given to all people with HIV to prevent Pneumocystis pneumonia and other infections; continued in TB/HIV co-infection.
- Isoniazid Preventive Therapy (IPT) for 6–36 months prevents TB in people with HIV without active TB.
