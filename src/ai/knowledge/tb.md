# Tuberculosis (TB)

## Overview
Tuberculosis is a bacterial infection caused by *Mycobacterium tuberculosis*. It most commonly affects the lungs (pulmonary TB) but can affect other parts of the body (extrapulmonary TB). TB is curable and preventable.

## Cause
- Caused by *Mycobacterium tuberculosis*, spread via airborne droplets when an infected person coughs, sneezes, or talks.
- Not everyone infected with TB bacteria becomes sick; latent TB infection (LTBI) has no symptoms and is not contagious.

## Symptoms
Pulmonary TB symptoms:
- Cough lasting **more than 2 weeks** (with or without sputum, may be blood-stained)
- Night sweats (drenching)
- Unexplained weight loss
- Fever (low-grade, especially in the afternoon/evening)
- Chest pain or difficulty breathing
- Fatigue or weakness
- Loss of appetite

Extrapulmonary TB symptoms vary by site (e.g., swollen lymph nodes in neck, abdominal pain, headache and neck stiffness in TB meningitis).

## Diagnosis
- **Sputum smear microscopy:** Examine sputum under microscope for acid-fast bacilli (AFB). Simple, low-cost, widely available.
- **GeneXpert MTB/RIF:** Molecular test that detects TB DNA and rifampicin resistance in under 2 hours. Recommended as initial diagnostic test by WHO. Available in major Nigerian hospitals and TB reference labs.
- **Chest X-ray:** Shows abnormalities suggestive of TB (cavities, infiltrates, lymphadenopathy). Often used as a screening tool.
- **Culture:** Gold standard, takes 2–6 weeks; used for drug susceptibility testing.
- **LAM urine test:** For people with advanced HIV (CD4 <100) to detect TB.

## Treatment
Standard 6-month regimen:
- **Intensive phase (2 months):** Rifampicin + Isoniazid + Pyrazinamide + Ethambutol (RHZE) — daily fixed-dose combination.
- **Continuation phase (4 months):** Rifampicin + Isoniazid (RH) — daily.

- Treatment is directly observed (DOT) — the patient swallows medication in front of a healthcare worker or trained community volunteer.
- Fixed-dose combination tablets reduce pill burden and improve adherence.
- Never miss a dose; defaulting treatment leads to relapse and drug resistance.

## DOTS (Directly Observed Therapy, Short-course)
WHO-recommended strategy with 5 components:
1. Political commitment with sustained funding
2. Case detection through quality-assured bacteriology
3. Standardized short-course chemotherapy under direct observation
4. Effective drug supply and management
5. Monitoring and evaluation system

In Nigeria, DOTS is delivered through:
- Designated DOTS centres in public health facilities
- Community DOTS through trained volunteers
- Private provider DOTS

## MDR-TB (Multi-Drug Resistant TB)
- Resistance to at least rifampicin and isoniazid, the two most powerful first-line drugs.
- Causes: inadequate or incorrect treatment, treatment interruption, poor drug quality, transmission of resistant strains.
- Diagnosis: GeneXpert detects rifampicin resistance; culture and DST confirm MDR.
- Treatment: 9–20 months with second-line drugs (fluoroquinolones, injectables, bedaquiline, linezolid, delamanid).
- Treatment is more complex, longer, costlier, and has more side effects.

## TB/HIV Co-Infection
- All TB patients should be tested for HIV; all people with HIV should be screened for TB.
- People with HIV are 18 times more likely to develop active TB.
- TB is the leading cause of death among people with HIV.
- Co-treatment: Start ART within 2–8 weeks of starting TB treatment. DTG dose adjustment needed with rifampicin (extra 50 mg).
- Cotrimoxazole Preventive Therapy (CPT) reduces mortality in TB/HIV co-infected patients.
- Isoniazid Preventive Therapy (IPT) is given to people with HIV after active TB is ruled out.

## Prevention
- BCG vaccine — given at birth in Nigeria; protects against severe forms of TB in children (TB meningitis, miliary TB).
- Infection control: cough etiquette, ventilation, mask-wearing in health facilities.
- Contact tracing: Screen household contacts; put child contacts on IPT after ruling out active TB.
- Improve nutrition, housing, and reduce overcrowding.
