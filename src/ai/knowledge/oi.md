# Opportunistic Infections (OIs) in HIV

## Overview
Opportunistic infections are infections that occur more frequently and are more severe in people with weakened immune systems, such as people living with HIV with low CD4 counts. OIs are a sign that HIV has progressed. With effective ART and a healthy immune system, OI risk is significantly reduced.

## Oral Thrush (Candidiasis)
- **Cause:** *Candida* fungus (usually *C. albicans*)
- **Symptoms:** White, creamy patches on tongue, inner cheeks, roof of mouth, or throat; redness, pain, bleeding when scraping; difficulty swallowing (if oesophageal candidiasis)
- **When CD4:** Usually <200 cells/mm³ (or <100 for oesophageal)
- **Treatment:** Fluconazole 200 mg daily for 7–14 days (oral); clotrimazole lozenges for mild cases. Oesophageal requires fluconazole 200–400 mg daily for 14–21 days.
- **When to seek care:** At the first sign of white patches in mouth or difficulty swallowing.

## Pneumocystis Pneumonia (PCP)
- **Cause:** *Pneumocystis jirovecii* fungus
- **Symptoms:** Gradual onset of fever, dry cough, progressive shortness of breath, chest tightness. Oxygen levels may be low.
- **When CD4:** Typically <200 cells/mm³
- **Diagnosis:** Chest X-ray (ground-glass opacities), clinical presentation, induced sputum or BAL
- **Treatment:** High-dose cotrimoxazole (TMP-SMX) for 21 days + oral corticosteroids if hypoxic. Alternative: pentamidine, dapsone, clindamycin + primaquine.
- **Prevention:** Cotrimoxazole Preventive Therapy (CPT) when CD4 <200 or WHO Stage 3/4.
- **When to seek care:** Any person with HIV and CD4 <200 who develops fever with dry cough and difficulty breathing.

## Cryptococcal Meningitis
- **Cause:** *Cryptococcus neoformans* fungus (found in soil, bird droppings)
- **Symptoms:** Headache (persistent, worsening), fever, neck stiffness, photophobia, confusion, altered mental status, seizures.
- **When CD4:** Usually <100 cells/mm³
- **Diagnosis:** Lumbar puncture with CSF cryptococcal antigen (CrAg) testing; serum CrAg screening.
- **Treatment:** Induction: Amphotericin B + flucytosine (or fluconazole 1200 mg/day if flucytosine not available) for 2 weeks; Consolidation: fluconazole 800 mg/day for 8 weeks; Maintenance: fluconazole 200 mg/day.
- **Prevention:** Fluconazole prophylaxis for people with HIV with CD4 <100 and CrAg positive.
- **When to seek care:** URGENT — any person with HIV and CD4 <100 with persistent headache, fever, or confusion requires immediate evaluation. Cryptococcal meningitis is fatal without treatment.

## Toxoplasmosis (Cerebral Toxoplasmosis)
- **Cause:** *Toxoplasma gondii* parasite
- **Symptoms:** Headache, focal neurological deficits (weakness on one side, speech changes), fever, confusion, seizures.
- **When CD4:** Usually <100 cells/mm³
- **Diagnosis:** CT or MRI (ring-enhancing brain lesions) + clinical presentation + serology (IgG positive)
- **Treatment:** Pyrimethamine + sulfadiazine + folinic acid for 6 weeks; then maintenance therapy until CD4 >200.
- **Alternative:** Trimethoprim-sulfamethoxazole (cotrimoxazole) can be used.
- **Prevention:** CPT covers toxoplasmosis. Avoid undercooked meat; wash hands after handling cat litter.
- **When to seek care:** Any new focal neurological symptoms in a person with HIV and low CD4.

## Cytomegalovirus (CMV)
- **Cause:** CMV virus (a herpesvirus)
- **Symptoms:** Most commonly CMV retinitis — blurred vision, floaters, flashing lights, loss of peripheral vision, blindness. Can also cause colitis, oesophagitis, pneumonitis.
- **When CD4:** Usually <50 cells/mm³
- **Diagnosis:** Fundoscopic eye exam (retinal changes — "tomato sauce & ketchup" appearance); biopsy for other sites.
- **Treatment:** Valganciclovir or ganciclovir; foscarnet if resistant. Usually requires lifelong maintenance until CD4 >100–150 on ART.
- **When to seek care:** Any visual changes (floaters, blurring, flashing lights) in a person with HIV and very low CD4.

## Mycobacterium Avium Complex (MAC)
- **Cause:** *Mycobacterium avium* complex (non-tuberculous mycobacteria)
- **Symptoms:** Fever, night sweats, weight loss, fatigue, diarrhoea, abdominal pain, anaemia, hepatosplenomegaly.
- **When CD4:** Usually <50 cells/mm³
- **Diagnosis:** Blood culture, bone marrow culture, lymph node biopsy.
- **Treatment:** Clarithromycin or azithromycin + ethambutol + rifabutin (or other agents).
- **Prevention:** Azithromycin 1200 mg weekly or clarithromycin 500 mg twice daily when CD4 <50.
- **When to seek care:** Persistent fever, weight loss, and abdominal symptoms with low CD4.

## General Prevention of OIs
- Start ART early (the best prevention is a strong immune system)
- Cotrimoxazole Preventive Therapy (CPT) for all people with HIV with CD4 <350 or WHO Stage 3/4
- Fluconazole prophylaxis for cryptococcal disease if CrAg positive and CD4 <100
- Azithromycin prophylaxis for MAC if CD4 <50
- Vaccinations: pneumococcal, influenza, HBV, HPV, tetanus
- Good nutrition and safe water
- Regular clinic attendance and monitoring
