# Post-Exposure Prophylaxis (PEP)

## Overview
Post-exposure prophylaxis (PEP) is the use of antiretroviral medicines after a possible exposure to HIV to prevent infection. PEP is an emergency measure — it must be started within 72 hours of exposure and taken for 28 days.

## What PEP Is
- A 28-day course of antiretroviral medication (usually TLD — TDF + 3TC + DTG, one pill once daily).
- PEP is not a substitute for regular HIV prevention (condoms, PrEP).
- PEP is effective but not 100% — effectiveness decreases the later it is started.
- PEP is available free of charge in Nigerian public health facilities for occupational exposures.

## When to Take PEP
PEP is recommended after **high-risk exposure** to HIV:

### Sexual Exposure
- Receptive or insertive anal or vaginal sex without a condom with a partner known to have HIV or at high risk
- Condom breakage or slippage during sex with a high-risk partner
- Sexual assault (rape or defilement)

### Occupational Exposure (Healthcare Workers)
- Needle-stick injury with a needle used on an HIV-positive patient
- Splash of blood or body fluids onto mucous membranes (eyes, mouth) or non-intact skin (cuts, wounds)

### Other Exposures
- Sharing needles or syringes with someone who has HIV or is at high risk
- Direct contact of broken skin or mucous membranes with HIV-contaminated blood or body fluids

### Timing: Within 72 Hours
- **Start as soon as possible** — ideally within 2 hours of exposure.
- PEP is not effective if started after 72 hours.
- Every hour matters — do not delay.

## PEP Regimen

### Standard Regimen in Nigeria
**TLD (TDF 300 mg + 3TC 300 mg + DTG 50 mg)** — one tablet once daily for 28 days.

### Alternative Regimens
- If TDF is contraindicated (renal disease): AZT + 3TC + DTG or AZT + 3TC + LPV/r
- If the source has known drug resistance: Regimen selected based on resistance profile (consult HIV specialist)

### Support Medications
- Anti-emetics (if nausea occurs)
- Emergency contraceptive pills (for sexual assault survivors)

## Adherence to PEP
- **Take every dose for 28 days** — missing doses reduces effectiveness.
- Use alarms, reminders, pill boxes.
- Side effects (nausea, headache, diarrhoea, fatigue) are common but usually mild and resolve within the first week.
- If side effects are severe, do not stop — your provider can change the regimen.
- Complete the full 28-day course even if you feel fine.

## Follow-Up Testing Schedule

| Time Point | Test | Purpose |
|------------|------|---------|
| **Baseline** | HIV rapid test | Confirm HIV-negative at time of exposure |
| **6 weeks post-exposure** | HIV rapid test (or 4th-gen test) | Early detection if infection occurred |
| **3 months (12 weeks) post-exposure** | HIV rapid test (or 4th-gen test) | Final confirmation — 99.9% accurate |
| **Consider 6-month test** | HIV test | Only if source had drug-resistant HIV (rarely needed) |

- HIV test must be done at baseline to confirm the person was HIV-negative before starting PEP.
- While waiting for final test results, use condoms and avoid sharing needles to prevent possible onward transmission.
- Do not donate blood during the PEP period and follow-up.

## PEP in Special Situations

### Sexual Assault
- **Immediate care:** Emergency medical treatment, forensic examination (with consent), emergency contraception, STI prophylaxis, hepatitis B vaccine (if not vaccinated), PEP if indicated.
- **Psychosocial support:** Crisis counselling, mental health referral, legal support.
- **Follow-up:** Ensure continuation of PEP; repeat HIV testing at 6 weeks and 3 months; STI screening; mental health follow-up.
- PEP should be offered routinely to survivors of sexual assault in Nigerian healthcare settings per National Guidelines.

### Occupational Exposure (Healthcare Workers)
- SOP in every Nigerian hospital: HIV PEP protocol.
- Immediate first aid: Wash wound with soap and water; flush mucous membranes with water/saline.
- Report to occupational health or designated PEP focal person.
- Baseline HIV test for the exposed worker (source patient testing also needed with consent).
- Start PEP within 2 hours if source is HIV-positive or status unknown and high risk.
- Workers' compensation should cover PEP and follow-up.

### Children (Paediatric PEP)
- PEP is indicated for children after sexual abuse, needle-stick injury, or breastfeeding from untreated HIV-positive mother.
- Dosing is weight-based; DTG is approved for children ≥4 weeks and ≥3 kg.
- Same 72-hour window and 28-day course.

### Pregnant or Breastfeeding Women
- PEP can be given to pregnant or breastfeeding women.
- TLD is safe in pregnancy and breastfeeding.
- Specialist consultation recommended.

## Common Questions About PEP

### Is PEP 100% effective?
No. PEP is highly effective when started within 72 hours and taken correctly for 28 days, but not 100%. Starting within 2 hours gives the best chance of preventing infection.

### Can I take PEP more than once?
Yes, PEP can be used multiple times if there are repeated exposures. However, if you have ongoing HIV risk (e.g., multiple sexual partners, serodiscordant relationship), PrEP (daily prevention) is more appropriate than repeated PEP courses.

### Do I need to test during PEP?
HIV testing during the PEP period may cause anxiety (testing positive before PEP is complete could indicate pre-existing infection, not PEP failure). Follow the recommended schedule: baseline, 6 weeks, 3 months.

### Can I have sex while on PEP?
Use condoms during sex while on PEP and until your final HIV test at 3 months confirms you are HIV-negative. This prevents possible onward transmission if PEP has not fully prevented infection.

### What about side effects?
Nausea is the most common side effect. Take PEP with food. If vomiting occurs within 1 hour of taking PEP, take another dose. Contact your provider if side effects are severe — do not stop PEP.

## Key Differences: PEP vs PrEP

| Feature | PEP | PrEP |
|---------|-----|------|
| **When to take** | After exposure (within 72 hours) | Before exposure (daily) |
| **Duration** | 28 days | Ongoing |
| **Purpose** | Emergency prevention | Regular prevention |
| **Who should use** | Anyone with a recent high-risk exposure | HIV-negative people at ongoing substantial risk |
| **Start** | As soon as possible after exposure | When prescribed (any time) |

## Key Counselling Points
- PEP is an emergency — do not wait. Go to a health facility immediately.
- Start within 72 hours, but the sooner the better — ideally within 2 hours.
- Take every dose for 28 days — missing doses reduces effectiveness.
- Side effects are common but manageable — do not stop.
- Use condoms while taking PEP and until your 3-month HIV test.
- PEP is available at most public health facilities in Nigeria — ask for the PEP focal person or ART clinic.
- If you need PEP frequently, ask your provider about PrEP — it may be more appropriate for you.
