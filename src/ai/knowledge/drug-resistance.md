# Drug Resistance

## Overview
HIV drug resistance occurs when HIV mutates (changes its genetic structure) and becomes less sensitive or completely resistant to one or more antiretroviral drugs. Resistance makes treatment less effective and limits future treatment options. It is a serious consequence of suboptimal treatment and poor adherence.

## Causes of Drug Resistance

### Poor Adherence
- Missing doses allows low-level viral replication, which gives the virus opportunities to mutate.
- **Intermittent adherence** (taking drugs irregularly) is even more likely to cause resistance than completely stopping.
- The virus replicates fastest when drug levels are too low to fully suppress it but high enough to create selective pressure.

### Suboptimal Regimen
- Inadequate drug potency or incorrect dosing.
- Drug–drug interactions that lower ARV concentrations (e.g., rifampicin with some ARVs).
- Using a single ARV drug (monotherapy) — always use combination therapy.
- Stock-outs or dispensing errors that lead to treatment interruptions.

### Transmitted Resistance
- A person can be infected with a drug-resistant strain of HIV (transmitted drug resistance).
- Prevalence of transmitted resistance in Nigeria is increasing, especially to NNRTIs.

### Prevention of Mother-to-Child Transmission (PMTCT)
- Single-dose nevirapine (no longer standard) was associated with high resistance rates.
- Current regimens (TLD) have a higher genetic barrier to resistance.

## Testing for Resistance

### Genotype Testing
- **What it is:** A blood test that looks for mutations in the HIV virus's genetic material (reverse transcriptase, protease, integrase genes).
- **When to test:**
  - At entry into care (baseline) — to check for transmitted resistance
  - When viral load is >200 copies/mL (or >1,000 copies/mL for some assays) after ≥6 months on ART
  - When switching regimens due to treatment failure
  - In pregnant women with detectable viral load
- **Availability in Nigeria:** Genotype testing is available at reference laboratories and through some research programmes. Cost and access remain challenges.
- **Interpretation:** Results show specific mutations that confer resistance, guiding the choice of a new regimen.

### Phenotype Testing
- Directly measures how well the virus grows in the presence of drugs.
- More expensive and less commonly used than genotype testing.

## Management of Drug Resistance

### If Resistance Is Detected
- **Switch to a new, effective regimen** — typically a protease inhibitor-based or integrase inhibitor-based regimen.
- The new regimen should include at least 2, preferably 3, fully active drugs.
- Factors to consider: resistance profile, drug interactions, tolerability, co-morbidities, availability, cost.

### Common Switch Strategies

| Current Regimen | Resistance Pattern | Recommended Switch |
|-----------------|--------------------|--------------------|
| TDF+3TC+DTG (TLD) | Integrase resistance (rare) | Protease inhibitor-based (e.g., LPV/r + 2 NRTIs) |
| AZT+3TC+EFV | NNRTI + NRTI resistance | DTG-based + active NRTI backbone |
| TDF+3TC+EFV | NNRTI + NRTI resistance | DTG-based + active NRTI backbone |
| Any failing regimen | Multi-class resistance | Individualized based on genotype |

### Drug Resistance in Nigeria
- NNRTI resistance is increasing (especially to nevirapine and efavirenz).
- DTG has a high genetic barrier to resistance, making resistance less frequent.
- NRTI resistance (particularly to TDF) can occur with long-term treatment failure.
- Nigeria follows WHO recommendations for transitioning to DTG-based first-line regimens to reduce resistance.

## Prevention of Drug Resistance

### At the Individual Level
- **Adherence, adherence, adherence:** Take medications every day at the same time.
- Never miss a dose — use pill boxes, alarms, adherence support.
- Attend all clinic appointments.
- Report side effects to your healthcare provider — do not just stop the medication.
- Avoid drug interactions — tell your provider about all medications and herbal remedies you take.
- Keep your viral load suppressed — when the virus is undetectable, it cannot mutate.

### At the Health System Level
- Ensure reliable supply of quality ARVs (no stock-outs).
- Use fixed-dose combination tablets to simplify dosing.
- Test-and-treat approach — starting ART early at high CD4 reduces resistance risk.
- Routine viral load monitoring to detect treatment failure early.
- Provide adherence counselling at every visit.
- Conduct surveillance of transmitted and acquired resistance to guide national treatment protocols.

## Key Counselling Points
- Drug resistance is a serious problem — it means your medicines are not working as well.
- The best way to prevent resistance is to take your ART every day.
- If you develop resistance, there are other medicines available — but treatment becomes more complicated.
- Baseline resistance testing is helpful but not always available.
- Do not stop your ART if you feel fine — stopping gives the virus a chance to become resistant.
- If you are having trouble taking your medicines, tell your provider right away — do not wait until you become sick.
