# Malaria — Knowledge Base

## Transmission
Malaria is transmitted through the bite of infected female Anopheles mosquitoes. Transmission is highest during rainy seasons.

## Types
- Plasmodium falciparum (most common and dangerous in Nigeria)
- Plasmodium vivax, ovale, malariae (less common)

## Symptoms
- Fever (may be cyclical)
- Chills and rigors
- Headache
- Muscle and joint pain
- Nausea and vomiting
- Diarrhea
- Fatigue

## Severe Malaria Danger Signs
- Confusion or altered consciousness
- Seizures
- Difficulty breathing (respiratory distress)
- Severe anemia
- Jaundice (yellow eyes/skin)
- Dark urine (blackwater fever)
- Not able to eat or drink

## Diagnosis
- Malaria Rapid Diagnostic Test (RDT) — available at most clinics
- Microscopy (blood smear) — gold standard
- Test before treatment — do not treat based on symptoms alone

## Treatment
- Uncomplicated malaria: Artemisinin-based Combination Therapy (ACT)
  - Artemether-lumefantrine (Coartem) — most common
  - Artesunate-amodiaquine
  - Dihydroartemisinin-piperaquine
- Severe malaria: IV artesunate (hospital setting)
- Always complete the full course (typically 3 days)

## Prevention
- Insecticide-treated bed nets (ITNs) — sleep under one every night
- Indoor residual spraying (IRS)
- Mosquito repellents (DEET, picaridin)
- Remove standing water around the home
- Wear long sleeves and pants in the evening
- Intermittent preventive treatment in pregnancy (IPTp) — sulfadoxine-pyrimethamine at ANC visits

## Special Populations
- Pregnant women: malaria increases risk of maternal anemia, miscarriage, low birth weight
- Children under 5: highest risk of severe malaria and death
- People with HIV: higher risk of severe malaria if immunosuppressed

## Epidemiology (Nigeria)
- Nigeria accounts for approximately 27% of global malaria cases
- Malaria is endemic across all states
- Peak transmission: May–October (rainy season)

## Drug Resistance
- Partial resistance to artemisinin has been reported in Southeast Asia
- ACT remains effective in Nigeria
- Always complete the full treatment course to prevent resistance
