# Mental Health for Adolescents Living with HIV

## Overview
Mental health is a critical component of overall health for adolescents living with HIV (ALHIV). Adolescents face unique psychological challenges related to HIV, puberty, identity formation, and social relationships. Untreated mental health issues can affect ART adherence, clinic attendance, and overall health outcomes.

## Common Mental Health Issues in ALHIV

### Depression
- **Prevalence:** Depression is 2–3 times more common in ALHIV than in HIV-negative peers.
- **Symptoms (lasting ≥2 weeks):**
  - Persistent sadness, emptiness, or hopelessness
  - Loss of interest in activities once enjoyed
  - Changes in appetite or weight (too much or too little)
  - Sleep disturbances (insomnia or oversleeping)
  - Fatigue or low energy
  - Difficulty concentrating or making decisions
  - Thoughts of death or suicide
  - Irritability or anger
  - Withdrawal from family and friends
- **Impact:** Depression is a major predictor of poor ART adherence and viral failure.
- **Management:** Cognitive-behavioural therapy (CBT), interpersonal therapy, antidepressant medication (fluoxetine is commonly used in adolescents), support groups.

### Anxiety
- **Symptoms:** Excessive worry, restlessness, muscle tension, racing heart, sweating, trembling, dizziness, difficulty sleeping, avoidance of social situations.
- **Common triggers in ALHIV:** Disclosure concerns, clinic visits, blood draws, fear of telling partners, academic pressure, bullying.
- **Management:** Relaxation techniques, breathing exercises, counselling, CBT. Severe cases may benefit from medication.

### Substance Use
- **Common substances among Nigerian adolescents:** Alcohol, cannabis (marijuana/igbo), codeine-containing cough syrups, tramadol, tobacco.
- **Why ALHIV may use substances:** To cope with emotional pain, stigma, peer pressure, boredom, mental health symptoms.
- **Impact of substance use:**
  - Impaired judgment → risky sexual behaviour
  - Missed ART doses → viral failure and resistance
  - Drug interactions (alcohol affects liver function; cannabis can affect appetite and motivation)
  - Increased HIV transmission risk
- **Management:** Brief intervention, motivational interviewing, referral to substance use services, youth-friendly drug treatment programmes.

### Stigma and Discrimination
- **Internalized stigma:** Feelings of shame, guilt, worthlessness about having HIV.
- **Enacted stigma:** Discrimination, gossip, exclusion, verbal abuse from others.
- **Anticipated stigma:** Fear of being treated differently, leading to secrecy and social withdrawal.
- **Impact of stigma:**
  - Non-disclosure → lack of social support
  - Missed clinic visits (fear of being seen at HIV clinic)
  - Poor adherence (taking pills secretly, skipping doses)
  - Depression, anxiety, isolation
- **Management:** Peer support, disclosure counselling, community sensitization, school-based anti-stigma programmes.

### Disclosure Stress
- Adolescence is when many learn their HIV status (if perinatally infected) or choose to disclose to others.
- **Disclosure to the adolescent (telling them their status):** Should be a planned, gradual process starting in childhood; full disclosure by age 10–12.
- **Disclosure to others (by the adolescent):**
  - Major source of anxiety — fear of rejection, gossip, stigma
  - Most disclose to: mother, close friend(s), sexual partner
  - Least disclose to: father, extended family, teachers, classmates
- **Risks of non-disclosure:** No support for adherence, cannot take medicines openly, cannot have full relationships.
- **Benefits of measured disclosure:** Emotional and practical support, freedom, reduced secrecy burden.

### Grief and Loss
- Many ALHIV have experienced the death of one or both parents from AIDS-related illnesses.
- **Complicated grief:** Persistent, intense grief that impairs daily functioning.
- **Loss of expected future:** Some adolescents struggle with fears about their own mortality, especially if they have been sick.
- **Management:** Grief counselling, peer groups where adolescents share experiences, creative expression (writing, art, music).

## Recognizing When an Adolescent Needs Help
**Red flags that warrant referral to a mental health professional:**
- Talking about wanting to die or kill themselves
- Plan or means to harm themselves
- Extreme social withdrawal (refusing to leave room, quit school)
- Complete refusal to take ART
- Significant weight loss or gain
- Substance use that interferes with daily life
- Hallucinations or delusions
- Intense anger or aggression
- History of trauma (sexual abuse, physical abuse)

## Available Support

### In Healthcare Settings (Nigeria)
- **Adherence counsellors:** Trained to provide basic mental health support at HIV clinics
- **Social workers:** Can help with disclosure, family issues, school issues
- **Psychologists/Psychiatrists:** Available in tertiary hospitals and some secondary facilities; referral may be needed
- **Youth-friendly clinics:** Some Nigerian hospitals have dedicated adolescent/youth corners with trained staff

### Community and Peer Support
- **Community adherence groups (CAGs):** Peer support groups where adolescents meet regularly
- **Adolescent peer educators and mentors:** Trained ALHIV who support others
- **Youth networks:** Association of Positive Youth in Nigeria (APYIN), networks of young people living with HIV
- **Support groups in schools (where available):** Some schools have health clubs

### Helplines and Online Resources
- **Nigeria's Ministry of Health toll-free line:** 6222 (for HIV-related questions)
- **FHI 360/Nigeria adolescent programmes**
- **APIN Public Health Initiatives:** Adolescent support programmes
- **UNICEF Nigeria:** Youth engagement and mental health programmes

## Building Resilience in ALHIV
- **Life skills training:** Decision-making, problem-solving, communication, assertiveness, negotiation
- **Future orientation:** Education and vocational support, career guidance, scholarships where available
- **Positive identity:** Helping adolescents see themselves as more than their diagnosis
- **Social connection:** Keeping them linked to peers, family, mentors, and community
- **Self-care:** Encouraging hobbies, exercise, rest, spiritual support in ways that are meaningful to them

## What Healthcare Providers Can Do
- Ask about mood, sleep, appetite at every visit
- Screen for depression and anxiety (use PHQ-9, GAD-7, or local screening tools)
- Provide brief counselling or supportive listening
- Connect adolescents to peer support
- Refer to mental health specialists when needed
- Ensure confidentiality and create a non-judgemental environment
- Make clinic visits adolescent-friendly (flexible hours, same-day appointments, comfortable space)

## Key Counselling Points for Adolescents
- Your feelings are valid and important — it is okay to not be okay sometimes.
- You are not alone — many adolescents living with HIV face similar challenges.
- Taking care of your mental health is as important as taking your ART.
- There is support available — you do not have to go through this alone.
- It gets better — with the right support, you can live a full, happy life.
- If you ever feel like hurting yourself, tell someone you trust immediately.
