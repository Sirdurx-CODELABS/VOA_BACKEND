# Sexually Transmitted Infections (STIs)

## Overview
STIs are infections spread through sexual contact. They are common among sexually active adolescents and adults. Many STIs are curable; others are manageable. Untreated STIs can lead to serious complications including infertility, chronic pain, and increased HIV transmission risk. Having an STI increases the risk of acquiring or transmitting HIV.

## General Principles
- Many STIs can be asymptomatic, especially in women.
- Testing and treatment should include partner notification and treatment.
- STI treatment should be completed fully, even if symptoms resolve.
- Condoms are highly effective at preventing most STIs when used correctly and consistently.
- In Nigeria, STI services are available at public health facilities, some pharmacies, and youth-friendly clinics.

## Gonorrhoea
- **Cause:** *Neisseria gonorrhoeae* bacterium
- **Symptoms in men:** Urethritis — burning pain when urinating, pus discharge from penis
- **Symptoms in women:** Often asymptomatic; may have vaginal discharge, pain on urination, lower abdominal pain
- **Complications:** Pelvic inflammatory disease (PID), infertility, ectopic pregnancy, disseminated infection
- **Testing:** Gram stain (gram-negative diplococci in neutrophils), NAAT (nucleic acid amplification test), culture
- **Treatment:** Ceftriaxone 250 mg IM single dose + azithromycin 1 g oral single dose. Dual therapy covers possible co-infection with chlamydia.
- **Antimicrobial resistance:** Gonorrhoea is becoming resistant to many antibiotics; treatment may change based on sensitivity.

## Chlamydia
- **Cause:** *Chlamydia trachomatis* bacterium
- **Symptoms in men:** Urethral discharge, burning on urination (often milder than gonorrhoea)
- **Symptoms in women:** Often asymptomatic; may have mucopurulent discharge, intermenstrual bleeding, pelvic pain
- **Complications:** PID, infertility, ectopic pregnancy, Fitz-Hugh-Curtis syndrome (perihepatitis)
- **Testing:** NAAT (preferred), antigen tests, culture
- **Treatment:** Azithromycin 1 g single dose OR doxycycline 100 mg twice daily for 7 days

## Syphilis
- **Cause:** *Treponema pallidum* bacterium
- **Stages:**
  - **Primary:** Painless sore (chancre) at site of infection — resolves in 3–6 weeks even without treatment
  - **Secondary:** Rash (palms and soles), fever, sore throat, lymphadenopathy, condylomata lata — 2–12 weeks after primary
  - **Latent:** No symptoms but serology positive
  - **Tertiary (late):** Neurosyphilis, cardiovascular syphilis, gummas — years to decades after infection
- **Congenital syphilis:** Can cause stillbirth, neonatal death, deformities. Screen all pregnant women.
- **Testing:** RPR/VDRL (nontreponemal) + TPHA (treponemal) confirmatory
- **Treatment:** Benzathine penicillin G 2.4 million units IM single dose (primary, secondary, early latent); 3 doses at weekly intervals for late latent. Neurosyphilis: IV penicillin G for 10–14 days.
- **Jarisch-Herxheimer reaction:** Fever, chills, aches within 24 hours of treatment — self-limiting, treat symptomatically.

## Herpes Simplex Virus (HSV, Genital Herpes)
- **Cause:** HSV-2 (most common genital) or HSV-1
- **Symptoms:** Painful blistering sores/ulcers on genitals or perianal area; often preceded by tingling or burning. Fever and body aches during primary outbreak.
- **Recurrence:** Virus stays in body; outbreaks recur with triggers (stress, illness, sunlight)
- **Treatment:** Acyclovir 400 mg three times daily or valacyclovir 500 mg twice daily for 5 days (longer for primary). Suppressive therapy reduces recurrence and transmission.
- **No cure:** Antivirals manage but do not eliminate the virus.
- **HIV interaction:** Genital herpes increases HIV acquisition and transmission risk.

## HPV (Human Papillomavirus / Genital Warts)
- **Cause:** HPV types 6, 11 (low risk — warts); types 16, 18 (high risk — cancer)
- **Symptoms:** Flesh-coloured bumps/lesions on genitals, anus, mouth. Often painless. May be single or clustered.
- **Complications:** Cervical, vulvar, penile, anal, or oropharyngeal cancer (high-risk types)
- **Testing:** Clinical diagnosis for warts; Pap smear + HPV DNA for cervical screening
- **Treatment:** Cryotherapy, podophyllin cream, TCA application, electrocautery, surgical removal
- **Vaccination:** HPV vaccine (Gardasil) prevents most HPV types; recommended for girls and boys aged 9–14 in Nigeria's national immunization programme.
- **Prevention:** Condoms reduce but do not fully prevent HPV transmission.

## Trichomoniasis
- **Cause:** *Trichomonas vaginalis* parasite
- **Symptoms in women:** Frothy, yellowish-green, foul-smelling vaginal discharge; itching, burning, redness, pain on urination
- **Symptoms in men:** Usually asymptomatic; mild urethritis, discharge, burning
- **Testing:** Wet mount microscopy ("motile trichomonads"), NAAT, culture
- **Treatment:** Metronidazole 2 g single dose OR tinidazole 2 g single dose. Metronidazole 500 mg twice daily for 7 days may be more effective in HIV-positive women.
- **Partner treatment:** Essential to prevent reinfection.

## Prevention
- **Condoms (male and female):** Correct and consistent use prevents most STIs.
- **Circumcision:** Reduces HIV acquisition risk in men by ~60%; also reduces HPV, HSV-2.
- **Pre-exposure Prophylaxis (PrEP):** Prevents HIV but does not prevent other STIs.
- **Vaccination:** HPV vaccine, Hepatitis B vaccine.
- **Regular testing:** Especially if having multiple partners or new partner.
- **Mutual monogamy:** With a tested, uninfected partner.

## Partner Notification
- Inform sexual partners about STI diagnosis so they can be tested and treated.
- Can be done by the patient, or with assistance from healthcare provider (provider-assisted referral).
- Partner treatment helps prevent reinfection and further transmission.
- In Nigeria, partner notification services are offered at most HIV/STI clinics confidentially.

## When to Seek Care
- Any genital symptoms: discharge, sores, rash, lumps, burning, itching
- After unprotected sex with a new or casually known partner
- After sexual assault
- If a partner has been diagnosed with an STI
- Pregnant women should be tested for syphilis, HIV, and hepatitis B at first antenatal visit
