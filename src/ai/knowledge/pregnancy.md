# Pregnancy in HIV

## Overview
With effective ART, women living with HIV can have healthy pregnancies and give birth to HIV-free babies. Preventing mother-to-child transmission (PMTCT) is highly effective, with transmission rates below 5% (and as low as 1–2%) when prevention measures are fully implemented.

## PMTCT (Prevention of Mother-to-Child Transmission)

### How Mother-to-Child Transmission Occurs
During:
- **Pregnancy:** HIV can cross the placenta (accounts for 5–10% of transmissions)
- **Labour and delivery:** Exposure to maternal blood and vaginal secretions (accounts for 60–70% of transmissions)
- **Breastfeeding:** Through breast milk (accounts for 20–30% of transmissions)

### Transmission Risk Without Intervention
- Without any intervention: 15–45% risk of MTCT.
- With full PMTCT (ART + safe delivery + safe infant feeding): <5%.

### Option B+ (WHO/Nigeria Guidelines)
- **All pregnant and breastfeeding women with HIV start lifelong ART** regardless of CD4 count.
- Standard regimen: **TDF + 3TC + DTG** (TLD) — same as first-line for non-pregnant adults.
- ART is started as early as possible in pregnancy (ideally before conception or in the first trimester) and continued for life.
- The baby receives **prophylactic medication** after birth (nevirapine or AZT syrup for 4–12 weeks depending on risk).

| Timing | Mother | Infant |
|--------|--------|--------|
| During pregnancy | Start ART (TLD) | N/A |
| Labour | Continue ART; IV AZT if high viral load | Begin nevirapine immediately after birth |
| Postpartum | Continue ART for life | Nevirapine/AZT prophylaxis for 4–12 weeks |
| Breastfeeding | Continue ART throughout breastfeeding | Daily nevirapine during breastfeeding |

## ART During Pregnancy

### Safety of ARVs in Pregnancy
- **DTG (Dolutegravir):** Earlier concerns about neural tube defects (NTDs) with periconceptional DTG have not been confirmed by larger studies. DTG is now recommended by WHO as the first-line choice in pregnancy (benefits outweigh minimal risks).
- **TDF (Tenofovir):** Safe in pregnancy.
- **3TC (Lamivudine):** Safe in pregnancy.
- **EFV (Efavirenz):** Previously contraindicated in first trimester; now considered safe. However, DTG is preferred.

### Viral Load Monitoring in Pregnancy
- Viral load should be checked:
  - At first antenatal visit (baseline)
  - At 34–36 weeks (to determine delivery plan)
  - At delivery (if high risk)
  - Every 3 months during breastfeeding
- **Target:** Undetectable (<50 copies/mL) at delivery.

### If Viral Load Is Detectable at Delivery
- Consider elective caesarean section at 38 weeks to reduce transmission risk.
- Give IV AZT during labour.
- Give baby enhanced prophylaxis (AZT + nevirapine for 12 weeks).
- Advise exclusive formula feeding (with discussion of risks/benefits).

## Safe Delivery

### Vaginal Delivery (If Viral Load is Undetectable or <1,000 copies/mL)
- Vaginal delivery is safe.
- Avoid invasive procedures (scalp electrodes, instrumental delivery) unless medically necessary.
- Continue ART during labour. If the woman cannot swallow tablets, IV AZT is used.
- Membrane rupture to delivery should be as short as possible (<4 hours ideally).

### Caesarean Section (If Viral Load >1,000 copies/mL or Unknown)
- Elective caesarean section at 38 weeks reduces MTCT risk.
- Give IV AZT 3 hours before surgery.
- Continue ART.

## Infant Feeding

### Exclusive Breastfeeding (Recommended in Nigeria)
- WHO recommends exclusive breastfeeding for the first 6 months when the mother is on ART and has undetectable viral load.
- **If the mother is on ART and adherent:** Risk of transmission through breast milk is <1%.
- **Exclusive breastfeeding** (nothing other than breast milk and medicines) reduces risk compared to mixed feeding (breast milk + other foods/water).
- Continue breastfeeding for 12 months or longer with ART.

### Exclusive Formula Feeding (Alternative)
- If:
  - Mother's viral load is high/detectable
  - Mother is not adherent to ART
  - Mother has access to clean water, formula, and can prepare it safely
  - Safe infant formula is affordable and sustainable
- **Risks of formula feeding:** Increased risk of diarrhoea, pneumonia, malnutrition — especially in low-resource settings.

### Mixed Feeding
- **Avoid mixed feeding** (breast milk + other foods/liquids) during the first 6 months — it increases the risk of MTCT and diarrhoea.

## Infant Testing (Early Infant Diagnosis)

| Age | Test | Purpose |
|-----|------|---------|
| **Birth to 6 weeks** | **HIV DNA PCR** | First test; detects HIV DNA in the blood |
| **6 weeks** | **HIV DNA PCR** | Routinely done at the 6-week immunization visit |
| **6 months** | **HIV DNA PCR** | Second test (or antibody rapid test if PCR unavailable) |
| **18 months** | **HIV rapid antibody test** | Final test; confirms the child is HIV-negative (if earlier tests negative and no breastfeeding exposure) |
| **6 weeks after cessation of breastfeeding** | **HIV DNA PCR** | If the child was breastfed, test after breastfeeding ends |

**If any test is positive:**
- Start ART immediately (within 2 weeks of sample collection).
- Refer to paediatric HIV clinic.

## Infant Prophylaxis

| Scenario | Infant Regimen | Duration |
|----------|---------------|----------|
| Mother on ART from early pregnancy, virally suppressed | Nevirapine once daily | 6 weeks |
| Mother started ART late or has detectable viral load | AZT + nevirapine twice daily | 12 weeks |
| Mother diagnosed during labour | AZT + nevirapine + dose of NVP | 12 weeks with possible 3rd drug |

## Family Planning for Women Living with HIV
- Women with HIV have the same family planning options as HIV-negative women.
- **All methods are safe:** Condoms, oral contraceptives, injectables, implants, IUDs, LAM.
- **Drug interactions:** EFV-based ART may reduce effectiveness of some hormonal contraceptives (implant, pill). Use additional barrier method.
- **TLD (DTG):** No significant interaction with hormonal contraceptives.
- Dual protection: Condoms + another method for both pregnancy and HIV/STI prevention.

## Postnatal Care
- Mother: Continue ART lifelong; viral load monitoring; CD4 monitoring if previously low; cervical cancer screening.
- Baby: Immunizations on schedule; growth monitoring; early infant diagnosis; cotrimoxazole prophylaxis (if HIV-exposed, start at 6 weeks until HIV is ruled out).
- Partner testing and linkage to care.

## Key Counselling Points
- You can have a healthy baby if you take your ART every day and follow PMTCT guidelines.
- Start ART as early as possible in pregnancy — the sooner you start, the lower the risk.
- Continue ART for life — do not stop after delivery.
- Exclusive breastfeeding with ART is safe and recommended in Nigeria.
- Bring your baby for HIV testing at 6 weeks and at 18 months.
- You can use family planning to space your children and plan for the future.
- Your HIV status is confidential — the healthcare team will not share it without your consent.
- There is no shame in having HIV and becoming pregnant. You deserve a healthy pregnancy.
