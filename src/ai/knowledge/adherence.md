# ART Adherence Strategies

## Overview
Adherence means taking your ART exactly as prescribed — the right dose, at the right time, every day. Good adherence is the single most important factor in successful HIV treatment. With ≥95% adherence, most people achieve viral suppression within 3–6 months.

## Why Adherence Is Critical
- **Keeps the virus suppressed** — undetectable viral load
- **Prevents drug resistance** — skipping doses allows the virus to mutate
- **Protects your immune system** — CD4 count rises
- **Prevents transmission** — U=U applies when you are undetectable
- **Improves long-term health** — near-normal life expectancy
- **Saves future treatment options** — resistance limits what will work for you

## Common Barriers to Adherence

| Barrier | Examples |
|---------|----------|
| **Forgetting** | Busy schedule, school/ work, competing demands |
| **Stigma** | Hiding pills from family, friends, roommates; fear of being seen at clinic |
| **Side effects** | Nausea, dizziness, insomnia, weight gain |
| **Disclosure challenges** | Cannot take pills openly; pressure to hide status |
| **Mental health** | Depression, anxiety, substance use |
| **Logistical issues** | Transportation to clinic, long queues, stock-outs |
| **Pill fatigue** | Tired of taking medication every day for years |
| **Feeling well** | Belief that ART is no longer needed because they feel fine |
| **Beliefs/misinformation** | Myths about ART, faith healing, traditional medicine |

## Adherence Strategies

### 1. Pill Reminders
- **Phone alarms:** Set a daily alarm on your phone for the same time each day.
- **Pill boxes/dosette boxes:** Fill a weekly pill box every Sunday — you can see if you have missed a dose.
- **Calendar:** Mark each dose on a wall calendar; tick each day after taking your pills.
- **Adherence apps:** Apps like MediSafe, MyTherapy, or simple medication reminder apps.
- **Buddy system:** Ask a trusted friend or family member to remind you.
- **Routine linking:** Take your pill with a daily routine activity (e.g., brushing teeth, breakfast, watching the news).

### 2. Dealing with Side Effects
- **Nausea:** Take ART with food; eat small frequent meals; ginger tea; avoid spicy/fatty foods.
- **Dizziness/fatigue:** Take ART at bedtime (if the regimen allows) until side effects subside.
- **Insomnia (especially with DTG):** Take in the morning if it keeps you awake at night.
- **Headache:** Stay hydrated; paracetamol if needed (not ibuprofen with TDF without checking with provider).
- **Nightmares (EFV):** Take in the morning or switch to DTG if persistent.
- **Never stop ART because of side effects** — report them to your provider who can manage them or switch your regimen.

### 3. Disclosure Support
- **Selective disclosure:** You do not need to tell everyone. Start with one safe, trusted person.
- **Practice what to say:** Role-play disclosure with a counsellor or peer.
- **Disclosure for support:** Having someone who knows your status can help with pill reminders and clinic attendance.
- **School/work disclosure:** Only if needed (e.g., need for private space to take medication).
- **Partner disclosure:** Encouraged for prevention and support; partner notification services available.

### 4. Community Adherence Groups (CAGs)
- Small groups (6–8 people living with HIV) who meet monthly for:
  - Group adherence support and sharing of strategies
  - Group collection of ART refills (one person collects for all)
  - Peer counselling and encouragement
  - Social support
- Benefits: Reduced clinic visits, built-in support network, reduced stigma, improved retention.
- Available in many Nigerian HIV clinics supported by PEPFAR/CDC.

### 5. Managing Stigma
- **Internalized stigma:** Counselling, peer support, affirmations — "HIV does not define me."
- **Enacted stigma:** Know your rights; report discrimination; seek safe spaces.
- **Secrecy strategies:** Use plain pill bottles; carry only the dose needed for the day; use a small bag/purse.
- **Safe spaces:** Find clinics, support groups, and friends where you are accepted.
- **Education:** Share accurate HIV information to combat myths in your community.

### 6. Simplifying Your Regimen
- Fixed-dose combinations (e.g., TLD — one pill once daily) make adherence easier.
- If you struggle with twice-daily dosing, ask about once-daily options.
- If pill size is a problem, ask if smaller tablets are available (e.g., DTG 50 mg is small).

### 7. Problem-Solving Specific Situations

| Situation | Strategy |
|-----------|----------|
| **Overnight trip** | Carry enough pills for the trip + 2 extra in case of delay |
| **Wedding/event** | Set alarm; carry a dose in your pocket/purse |
| **Forgetting while travelling** | Pack pills in carry-on (not checked luggage); keep a spare dose in your bag |
| **Running out** | Refill 1 week before you finish; know your refill date |
| **Stock-out at clinic** | Ask about bridging supply; call the clinic hotline; go to another facility |
| **Alcohol use** | Take ART before drinking (if you drink); set alarm earlier; do not skip because you drank |
| **Religious fasting** | Discuss with provider; some ARVs can be taken with a small amount of water/food; adjust timing |
| **Sickness/vomiting** | If you vomit within 1 hour of taking ART, take a new dose (if within 2 hours of regular time). After 2 hours, do not repeat. |

### 8. Adherence Counselling
Adherence counsellors (available at all PEPFAR-supported clinics in Nigeria):
- Provide one-on-one support
- Help identify personal barriers
- Develop an individual adherence plan
- Use motivational interviewing
- Review pill counts at each visit
- Offer home visits if needed

### 9. Peer Support
- Connect with other people living with HIV through:
  - Support groups at the clinic
  - Community-based organizations
  - Online support groups (HEARTH, APIN, APYIN)
  - One-on-one peer mentoring
- Hearing how others manage makes the challenges easier to handle.

### 10. Directly Observed Therapy (DOT)
- Taking ART in front of a healthcare worker, trained community volunteer, or family member.
- Used for people who have difficulty with self-administered therapy (e.g., adolescents, those with substance use issues, severe depression).
- DOT can be temporary until adherence is consistent.

## What to Do When You Miss a Dose

**Same day (remember within 12 hours):**
- Take the missed dose immediately.
- Take your next dose at the usual time.
- Do NOT take a double dose.

**Next day (more than 12 hours late):**
- Skip the missed dose entirely.
- Take your regular dose at the usual time.
- Do NOT take two pills.

**Multiple missed doses:**
- Restore your routine as soon as possible.
- Do NOT double up — just start again with a single dose.
- Contact your adherence counsellor or healthcare provider.
- You may need a viral load test to check for treatment failure.

## For Adolescents
- Adherence can be more challenging for adolescents due to:
  - Busy school schedules
  - Fear of disclosure to peers
  - Forgetfulness
  - Denial or rebellion
- Strategies that work for adolescents:
  - Phone alarms / medication apps
  - Peer adherence buddies
  - Youth-friendly clinic hours
  - School-based pill support (private space to take pills)
  - Motivational interviewing focused on their future goals (education, career, relationships)

## For Parents / Caregivers
- Help young children establish a daily pill-taking routine early.
- Use reward systems for young children.
- Gradually transition responsibility to the adolescent as they mature.
- Attend clinic visits with your child.
- Be open about HIV in an age-appropriate way.

## Tracking Your Adherence
- Every pill counts. Use a diary, app, or calendar.
- At each clinic visit, the counsellor may ask:
  - "How many doses did you miss in the last week / last month?"
  - "What was the longest gap between doses?"
- Be honest — your provider cannot help if they do not know the truth.
- A "missed dose" = any dose taken >2 hours late.

## Key Counselling Points
- ART is not a punishment — it is your ticket to a long, healthy life.
- One missed dose gives the virus a chance to multiply. Many missed doses give it a chance to become resistant.
- You are not alone — every person on ART struggles at some point. There is always help available.
- You are the most important member of your healthcare team. You are in charge of taking your medicines.
- Your health is worth fighting for — every pill you take is a step toward a brighter future.
