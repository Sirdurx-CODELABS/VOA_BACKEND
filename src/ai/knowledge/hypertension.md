# Hypertension — Knowledge Base

## Definition
- Blood pressure >= 140/90 mmHg on at least two separate occasions
- Pre-hypertension: 120-139 / 80-89 mmHg
- Stage 1 hypertension: 140-159 / 90-99 mmHg
- Stage 2 hypertension: >= 160 / >= 100 mmHg

## Risk Factors
- Family history of hypertension
- Age (risk increases with age)
- Overweight or obesity (BMI >= 25)
- High salt intake
- Low potassium intake
- Physical inactivity
- Excessive alcohol consumption
- Smoking
- Stress
- Chronic kidney disease
- Diabetes

## Symptoms
- Often no symptoms ("silent killer")
- Severe hypertension may cause: headache, dizziness, nosebleeds, blurred vision, chest pain, shortness of breath

## Management
**Lifestyle (foundation of treatment):**
- DASH diet: fruits, vegetables, whole grains, low-fat dairy, lean protein, reduced salt
- Salt reduction: less than 5g (1 teaspoon) per day
- Regular exercise: 30 min/day, 5 days/week moderate aerobic activity
- Weight loss: even 5% weight reduction can lower BP
- Limit alcohol: max 1 drink/day for women, 2 for men
- Stop smoking

**Medications (prescribed by clinician):**
- Thiazide diuretics (hydrochlorothiazide)
- ACE inhibitors (lisinopril, enalapril)
- ARBs (losartan, telmisartan)
- Calcium channel blockers (amlodipine, nifedipine)
- Beta-blockers (atenolol, metoprolol)

## Complications
- Stroke (hemorrhagic and ischemic)
- Heart attack (myocardial infarction)
- Heart failure
- Kidney disease (hypertensive nephropathy)
- Vision loss (hypertensive retinopathy)
- Peripheral artery disease

## Hypertensive Emergency
- BP >= 180/120 mmHg with organ damage symptoms
- Symptoms: severe headache, chest pain, shortness of breath, blurred vision, seizure, confusion
- Action: immediate hospital emergency care

## Monitoring
- Check BP at every clinic visit
- Home BP monitoring: rest 5 minutes, sit with back supported, feet flat, arm at heart level
- Record readings to share with clinician
