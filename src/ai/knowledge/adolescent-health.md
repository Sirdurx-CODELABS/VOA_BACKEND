# Adolescent Health

## Overview
Adolescence (ages 10–19) is a period of rapid physical, emotional, and social development. Adolescents living with HIV or at risk of HIV have specific health needs that require youth-friendly, confidential, and comprehensive services.

## Puberty
- **Physical changes:** Growth spurt, breast development (thelarche) in girls, testicular enlargement in boys, pubic hair, menarche (first period), voice changes, acne.
- **Emotional changes:** Mood swings, heightened sensitivity, increased self-awareness.
- **HIV considerations:**
  - Some ARVs (especially EFV) can affect sleep, mood — be aware.
  - DTG has been associated with weight gain, which can be distressing during puberty.
  - Body image concerns are common — adolescents on ART may need reassurance.
  - Growth should be monitored at every clinic visit; ART can affect growth velocity.

## Sexual Health

### The Menstrual Cycle
- First period (menarche) typically between ages 10–16 in Nigerian girls.
- Common issues: irregular periods, dysmenorrhoea (painful periods), premenstrual syndrome.
- HIV considerations: menstrual changes can affect ART timing/experience; some women report changes in menstrual pattern when starting or switching ART.

### Sexual Debut
- Many Nigerian adolescents become sexually active during the teenage years.
- Key discussion points: consent, condom use, STI prevention, pregnancy prevention, HIV disclosure to partners.
- Comprehensive sexuality education (CSE) improves outcomes — it does not increase sexual activity.

### STI Prevention and Testing
- Sexually active adolescents should be offered:
  - HIV testing services (with appropriate counselling)
  - STI screening (symptom-based or laboratory-based where available)
  - Condoms (male and female) and education on correct use
  - PrEP for HIV-negative adolescents at substantial risk

## Consent and Confidentiality

### Age of Consent in Nigeria
- **Age of consent for sexual activity:** 18 years (Child's Rights Act — not adopted by all states).
- **Age of consent for medical treatment:** Generally 18; however, emancipated/duly mature adolescents (16+) may give independent consent in some contexts.
- **Age of consent for HIV testing:** 16+ years can test without parental consent. Under 16 should have parental/guardian consent.

### Confidentiality
- Adolescents have a right to confidentiality in healthcare settings.
- Healthcare providers should:
  - Assure adolescents that their information is kept private
  - Explain limits of confidentiality (e.g., if someone is at risk of serious harm)
  - Maintain separate, confidential files where possible
  - Provide consultation time alone with the adolescent (without parent/caregiver)
  - Only share information with others on a need-to-know basis with adolescent's consent

### Rights to Healthcare in Nigeria
Every adolescent has the right to:
- Access health services without discrimination
- Receive information about their health and treatment
- Give or withhold consent for treatment (where legally recognized)
- Confidentiality and privacy
- Be treated with dignity and respect
- Access to contraception and reproductive health services (per national guidelines)
- Access to HIV testing, treatment, and care

These rights are protected by:
- The Constitution of the Federal Republic of Nigeria (1999)
- The African Charter on the Rights and Welfare of the Child
- The Child's Rights Act (2003) — ratified by some but not all states
- National HIV/AIDS policies and guidelines

## Peer Pressure

### Types of Peer Pressure
- **Direct:** Overt encouragement to engage in specific behaviours (e.g., "Try this cigarette")
- **Indirect:** Subtle influence through social norms and desire to fit in
- **Positive:** Encouraging healthy behaviours, academic achievement, staying in school

### Peer Pressure and HIV Risk
- Pressure to have sex before ready
- Pressure to not use condoms (e.g., "condoms don't feel good")
- Pressure to share needles or drink alcohol
- Pressure to skip clinic visits or stop taking ART (to keep HIV status secret)

### Managing Peer Pressure
- **Assertiveness skills:** Practice saying "no" firmly and respectfully
- **Choose friends wisely:** Surround yourself with people who respect your choices
- **Find allies:** Identify friends, family, or mentors who support you
- **Plan ahead:** Think about what you will say if pressured
- **Understand consequences:** Making choices for yourself, not others
- **Safe spaces:** Youth-friendly clinics, clubs, religious groups, sports teams

## Life Skills for Adolescents
Life skills help adolescents navigate challenges and make healthy decisions:

### Self-Awareness
- Knowing your strengths, weaknesses, values, and feelings
- Understanding how your HIV status fits into your identity
- Recognizing your goals and aspirations

### Communication
- Expressing thoughts and feelings clearly
- Asking for what you need from parents, providers, partners
- Negotiating condom use with a partner

### Decision-Making
- Weighing options and consequences
- Making choices aligned with your values and goals
- Taking ART even when you do not feel like it
- Choosing to disclose or not disclose your HIV status

### Problem-Solving
- Identifying problems, generating solutions, choosing a solution, evaluating outcomes

### Stress Management
- Recognizing signs of stress
- Using healthy coping strategies (exercise, talking to someone, hobbies)
- Avoiding harmful coping (substances, risky sex, isolation)

### Goal-Setting
- Setting achievable short-term and long-term goals
- Making plans for education, career, relationships, health

## Adolescent-Friendly Health Services
In Nigeria, adolescent-friendly services aim to provide:

### The "Youth-Friendly" Model
- **Accessible:** Convenient location, hours, and fees
- **Acceptable:** Respectful, non-judgemental, non-discriminatory care
- **Appropriate:** Services that meet the specific needs of adolescents
- **Effective:** Competent providers, evidence-based protocols
- **Confidential:** Privacy guaranteed

### What Adolescent-Friendly Clinics Offer
- HIV testing and counselling
- ART services
- STI screening and treatment
- Family planning/contraception
- Pregnancy testing and options counselling
- Mental health support
- Nutrition counselling
- Peer support groups
- Life skills training
- School re-entry support

### Finding Adolescent-Friendly Services in Nigeria
- Ask at your local general hospital or primary healthcare centre
- Look for "Youth Corner", "Adolescent Clinic", or "Youth-Friendly Centre" signs
- Many PEPFAR-supported sites have dedicated adolescent services
- Some states have youth-friendly centres in community settings

## Supporting Educational and Vocational Goals
- Being HIV-positive should not prevent an adolescent from attending school or pursuing their dreams.
- ART helps adolescents stay healthy enough to attend school regularly.
- Some adolescents may need support with:
  - Disclosure to school authorities (only if it helps the adolescent)
  - Managing pill-taking at school
  - Addressing HIV-related stigma at school
  - Accessing scholarships and educational support
  - Vocational training for those not in school

## Key Counselling Points
- Your body is changing — this is normal and healthy. If you have questions, ask your provider.
- You have the right to confidential healthcare and information.
- You have the right to say no to sex, no to drugs, no to anything that puts your health at risk.
- You are in charge of your health — take your ART because you want to take care of yourself.
- Peer pressure is real, but you can learn to manage it.
- There are youth-friendly clinics and support groups available — you are not alone.
- Being HIV-positive does not define you. You have a whole life ahead of you with dreams and goals to pursue.
