# Nutrition for People Living with HIV (PLHIV)

## Overview
Good nutrition is essential for people living with HIV. HIV increases the body's energy needs, and a balanced diet supports the immune system, helps ART work effectively, and maintains healthy body weight. Malnutrition accelerates HIV progression.

## Why Nutrition Matters for PLHIV
- HIV increases resting energy expenditure by 10–30%, even without symptoms.
- During OIs, energy needs increase further.
- A well-nourished body absorbs ART better and copes with side effects.
- Good nutrition supports CD4 recovery and reduces wasting.
- In children, nutrition is critical for growth and development.

## Balanced Diet Principles
A healthy plate should include foods from all food groups:

### 1. Energy-Giving Foods (Carbohydrates & Fats)
- Staples: pounded yam, garri, fufu, rice, bread, plantain, cassava, yam, sweet potatoes
- Healthy fats: palm oil (in moderation), groundnut oil, vegetable oil, avocados, groundnuts, cashew nuts, coconut
- These provide calories and energy, especially important during illness or weight loss

### 2. Body-Building Foods (Proteins)
- Animal sources: fish (fresh, smoked, dried), chicken, eggs, beef, goat meat, organ meats (liver in moderation)
- Plant sources: beans (ewa), soybeans, groundnuts, agushie (melon seeds), egusi, okpa, bambara nuts, locust beans (iru/dawadawa)
- Protein is critical for immune cell production and muscle maintenance

### 3. Protective Foods (Vitamins & Minerals)
- Fruits: oranges, mangoes, pawpaw (papaya), bananas, pineapples, guava, watermelon, apples, garden eggs
- Vegetables: ugwu (pumpkin leaves), bitter leaf, ewedu (jute leaves), okra, green leafy vegetables, carrots, tomatoes, peppers
- These provide antioxidants, vitamins A, C, E, and iron

### 4. Dairy/Alternatives
- Fresh milk, yogurt, cheese (wara)
- Soy milk (especially for those who are lactose intolerant, common in African populations)

## Key Micronutrients for PLHIV

| Micronutrient | Importance | Food Sources |
|---------------|-----------|-------------|
| **Iron** | Prevents anaemia; supports oxygen transport | Red meat, liver, dark leafy greens, beans, fortified cereals. Iron absorption improved by vitamin C (e.g., eating oranges with iron-rich foods) |
| **Zinc** | Supports immune function; wound healing | Oysters, meat, poultry, beans, nuts, whole grains, pumpkin seeds |
| **Selenium** | Antioxidant; supports immune system | Brazil nuts, fish, shellfish, eggs, sunflower seeds, mushrooms |
| **Vitamin A** | Maintains mucous membranes; immune function | Pawpaw, mango, carrots, pumpkin, dark leafy greens, red palm oil |
| **Vitamin C** | Antioxidant; wound healing; iron absorption | Oranges, guava, pawpaw, pineapple, green peppers, tomatoes |
| **Vitamin D** | Bone health; immune function | Sunlight (main source), fatty fish, egg yolks, fortified foods |
| **B-complex** | Energy metabolism; nerve function | Whole grains, beans, nuts, eggs, meat, leafy greens |

## Food Safety for PLHIV
People with low CD4 counts are at higher risk of foodborne infections. Follow food safety practices:
- **Wash hands** with soap and water before eating and preparing food
- **Wash fruits and vegetables** thoroughly with clean water (soak in salted water or vinegar water if possible)
- **Cook food thoroughly** — especially meat, fish, eggs
- **Avoid raw or undercooked foods** (raw eggs, raw meat, raw fish)
- **Drink safe water** — boiled, filtered, or bottled water; avoid sachet water of unknown source
- **Avoid cross-contamination** in the kitchen (separate cutting boards for raw meat and vegetables)
- **Store food properly** — cover to protect from flies; refrigerate if available

## Managing Side Effects That Affect Eating

| Side Effect | Nutritional Strategy |
|-------------|---------------------|
| **Nausea/vomiting** | Eat small frequent meals; avoid strong-smelling foods; eat dry crackers/bread before getting up; ginger tea; avoid fatty/fried foods |
| **Diarrhoea** | Increase fluids (clean water, pap/ogi, rice water); eat bananas, white rice, applesauce, toast (BRAT diet); avoid raw foods, spicy foods, excess fibre; oral rehydration solution if needed |
| **Loss of appetite** | Eat small meals 5–6 times a day; nutrient-dense foods; smoothies, soups, porridge; add dried milk powder or groundnuts for extra calories |
| **Weight loss/wasting** | Fortify foods: add groundnuts, soy, egg, milk powder, oil to meals; high-calorie shakes; eat more often |
| **Mouth sores** | Soft, moist foods; porridge, mashed potatoes, yogurt, smoothies; avoid spicy, salty, acidic foods; use a straw for liquids |
| **Metallic taste** (with some ARVs) | Use plastic utensils; rinse mouth with baking soda water; add lemon, herbs, spices |
| **Fatigue** | Easy-to-prepare foods; ask for help with cooking; nutrient-dense snacks |

## Nutrition and TB
- TB increases protein and energy requirements significantly.
- TB patients often experience severe weight loss and wasting.
- Nutritional support improves TB treatment outcomes.
- Zinc, vitamin D, vitamin A, and selenium are especially important in TB recovery.
- Ensure adequate calorie and protein intake during TB treatment.
- Food supplements (e.g., PlumpyNut, therapeutic foods) may be provided through TB/HIV programmes.

## Nutrition for Children with HIV
- Children need extra calories for both growth and fighting HIV.
- Continue breastfeeding (if HIV-negative mother or if mother is on ART and virally suppressed) or provide safe infant formula.
- Ensure timely introduction of complementary foods at 6 months.
- Monitor growth with growth charts at every clinic visit.
- Address any feeding difficulties early.
- Multivitamin supplementation is often recommended.

## What to Avoid
- **Raw or undercooked meat, fish, eggs** — risk of toxoplasmosis, salmonella
- **Unpasteurised dairy products** — risk of listeria, TB
- **Excess alcohol** — interferes with ART, liver function, and nutrition
- **Herbal remedies without medical advice** — some interact with ART
- **Excess salt and sugar** — contribute to hypertension and diabetes (PLHIV on ART may be at higher risk)
- **Unwashed fruits/vegetables** — risk of foodborne illness

## Key Counselling Points
- Eat a variety of foods from all food groups every day.
- Drink plenty of clean, safe water.
- Wash your hands and your food thoroughly.
- If you have trouble eating because of side effects, talk to your healthcare provider.
- Good nutrition is part of your HIV treatment — it helps ART work better.
- A dietitian or nutrition counsellor can help create a plan that works for you.
