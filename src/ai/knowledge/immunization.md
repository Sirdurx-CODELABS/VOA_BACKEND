# Immunization — Knowledge Base

## How Vaccines Work
Vaccines train the immune system to recognize and fight specific germs. They contain weakened or inactivated parts of germs that cannot cause disease but trigger an immune response.

## Vaccine Safety
- Vaccines go through rigorous testing before approval
- Common side effects: mild fever, soreness at injection site, fussiness — these are normal signs the immune system is responding
- Serious side effects: extremely rare (1 in millions)
- No link between vaccines and autism — this has been thoroughly studied and debunked

## Nigeria Routine Immunization Schedule
- **At birth**: BCG (TB), OPV-0 (polio), Hepatitis B (birth dose)
- **6 weeks**: Penta-1 (DPT-HepB-Hib), OPV-1, PCV-1, Rotavirus-1
- **10 weeks**: Penta-2, OPV-2, PCV-2, Rotavirus-2
- **14 weeks**: Penta-3, OPV-3, PCV-3, IPV (inactivated polio vaccine)
- **6 months**: Vitamin A (first dose)
- **9 months**: Measles-1, Yellow Fever
- **12 months**: Measles-2, Meningitis (MenACWY)
- **15 months**: Measles-2 (if not given at 12 months)
- **24 months**: Booster doses, Vitamin A

## Adolescent Vaccines
- HPV vaccine (girls 9-14): 2 doses, 6 months apart — prevents cervical cancer
- Tdap (tetanus, diphtheria, pertussis) booster: recommended at 11-12 years
- Meningitis vaccine: recommended for adolescents

## Adult Vaccines
- Tetanus booster: every 10 years
- Influenza (flu) shot: annually, especially for high-risk groups
- COVID-19: primary series + boosters as recommended
- Pneumococcal: for adults 65+ and high-risk individuals
- Hepatitis B: catch-up series for adults not vaccinated as children

## Travel Vaccines
- Yellow Fever: required for travel to endemic countries
- Meningitis: required for Hajj pilgrimage
- Typhoid: recommended for travel to high-risk areas
- Cholera: for travel to active outbreak areas
- Plan travel vaccines 4-6 weeks before departure

## Catch-up Vaccination
- Children who missed doses can be caught up
- No need to restart any series — just give missed doses
- Take child's immunization card to the health facility

## Vaccine-Preventable Diseases
- Polio: nearly eradicated globally, Nigeria is polio-free since 2020
- Measles: highly contagious, can be severe
- Yellow Fever: mosquito-borne, can cause liver failure
- Whooping Cough (pertussis): severe cough in infants
- Tetanus: from contaminated wounds
- Hepatitis B: can cause liver cancer
- HPV: causes cervical cancer
- Pneumonia: leading cause of child death
- Meningitis: brain infection, can be fatal
