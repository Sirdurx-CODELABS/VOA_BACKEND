# Child Health — Knowledge Base

## Newborn Care (0-28 days)
- Exclusive breastfeeding: on demand, 8-12 times per day
- Keep baby warm: skin-to-skin contact, appropriate clothing
- Cord care: keep dry and clean, no application of substances
- Danger signs: difficulty feeding, fever or low temperature, fast breathing, chest indrawing, convulsions, lethargy, jaundice (yellow eyes/skin), umbilical redness/pus
- When to seek care immediately: any danger sign, not feeding, vomiting everything

## Breastfeeding
- Exclusive breastfeeding for first 6 months (no water, no other foods)
- Benefits: provides complete nutrition, antibodies, bonding, reduces infection risk
- Continue breastfeeding with complementary foods up to 2 years
- Common challenges: sore nipples, low milk supply, engorgement — lactation counseling available at most clinics

## Growth Monitoring
- Weight: at birth, 6 weeks, 10 weeks, 14 weeks, 6 months, 9 months, 12 months, 18 months, 24 months
- Length/height
- Head circumference (up to 2 years)
- Plot on growth chart — steady growth is healthy
- Growth faltering (flat or falling curve): investigate nutrition, illness, feeding practices

## Development Milestones
- **2 months**: smiles, follows with eyes, coos
- **4 months**: laughs, reaches for objects, holds head steady
- **6 months**: sits with support, rolls over, responds to name
- **9 months**: sits without support, babbles, transfers objects
- **12 months**: stands with support, says mama/dada, waves bye
- **18 months**: walks independently, says several words, points to things
- **2 years**: runs, speaks 2-word sentences, follows simple instructions
- **3 years**: climbs, speaks in sentences, plays with others

## Common Childhood Illnesses

### Diarrhea
- Leading cause of child death in developing countries
- Key treatment: ORS (oral rehydration solution) + zinc supplementation
- Continue breastfeeding during diarrhea
- Danger signs: sunken eyes, skin pinch goes back slowly, unable to drink, bloody stool
- Vaccine: Rotavirus vaccine at 6 and 10 weeks

### Pneumonia
- Leading infectious cause of child death
- Signs: fast breathing, chest indrawing, fever, cough
- Treatment: antibiotics (amoxicillin), oxygen if severe
- Prevention: PCV vaccine, good nutrition, exclusive breastfeeding

### Malaria in Children
- Children under 5 at highest risk
- Fever + chills in malaria-endemic area = malaria until proven otherwise
- Test with RDT or blood smear before treatment
- ACT treatment (weight-based dosing by clinician)
- Danger signs: convulsions, difficulty breathing, inability to eat/drink, lethargy

### Malnutrition
- Acute malnutrition: wasting (low weight for height), edema
- Chronic malnutrition: stunting (low height for age)
- Management: therapeutic feeding, micronutrient supplementation, treat underlying illness
- Vitamin A supplementation at 6 months, 12 months, 18 months, 24 months

## Deworming
- Albendazole or mebendazole every 6 months starting at 12 months
- Reduces anemia, improves growth and school performance

## Integrated Management of Childhood Illness (IMCI)
WHO/UNICEF strategy for child health in low-resource settings:
- Classify illness by symptom
- Identify treatment
- Treat or refer
- Counsel mother
- Follow-up
